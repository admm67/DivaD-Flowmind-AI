# Throughput Accounting

Use system-level economic measures. Confirm company-specific accounting
definitions before calculations.

- Throughput (T): rate at which the system generates money through sales.
  A common simplified product approximation is selling price minus truly
  totally variable costs.
- Investment (I): money tied up in items intended for sale or to support
  future throughput, per the organization's policy.
- Operating Expense (OE): money spent to turn investment into throughput.

Common simplified measures:
- Net Profit = T - OE
- ROI = (T - OE) / I
- Productivity = T / OE
- Inventory Turns = T / I

## Constraint-minute decisions
Throughput per constraint unit = product throughput / constraint time.

Consider demand, sales mix, material and truly variable costs, constraint
time, setups, due dates, penalties, quality, and displacement of other orders.
Do not rank products only by gross margin per unit when constraint time differs.

Ask whether the order is incremental, whether spare constraint capacity exists,
what incremental throughput and OE are required, and what commitments or
strategic effects apply. Show assumptions; never fabricate cost inputs.
These measures are management lenses, not statutory financial statements.
